## Shared direction

Start with subject/action/setting and intended feeling. Preserve the user's idea. Camera, Tone, Lighting and Texture are linked scene-specific decisions, not four generic adjectives or a stock aesthetic catalog.

- Camera: device/capture language, projection/lens, viewpoint/distance, shot size/framing, focus and aspect. Film format differs from focal length; camera movement differs from zoom; fisheye differs from a short perspective lens.
- Tone: emotional read, subject/action/setting, story state, time and weather. Narrative contrast means unexpected situation, identity/attitude or emotion; it is separate from color contrast.
- Lighting: motivated source, direction, hard/soft quality/fill, atmosphere and color/Kelvin. Do not copy tutorial numbers as universal settings.
- Texture: material identity, localized wear, supporting small details and intentional capture finish. Multiple materials and wear coexist.

## Actual references, style and motion

At 12:49–13:44, source 2 supplies the robot image to both the planning model and generator. A text image description is not attachment. Assign source roles and verify identity in the output.

At 14:47–15:21 it extracts reusable style from a successful image AND its producing prompt. Keep fixed photographic traits separate from variable subject/action/location/props. A style file is context, not weight training. The linked downloaded night-fisheye companion recipe is not proven to be the exact response shown for the disposable-camera extraction.

At 17:51–19:22, human operator behaviors react to events and recover: handheld, crash zoom, concealed observation, whip pan, walking backward, obstruction, exposure adaptation and focus failure. Compatible operator techniques combine at 19:28–20:12. Distinguish a single coherent capture language from a timed combination of operator behaviors. Some sample video prompts also separate image/audio roles, voice traits, dialogue, subject relationships and sound.

## Comparisons and limits

Source 2 compares a fixed prompt across models at 01:30–08:35; realism, instruction following and text ability differ. The early video comparison intentionally has no reference; identity-preserving later examples do. It iterates 480p drafts then upscales a selected result 1080p around 07:47–08:09. These are historical/provider-specific examples, not current model rankings, pricing or universal resolutions.

Our palettes, aesthetic languages, generated option images, schema and native helpers are original adaptations. Useful extras—protected copies, editable prompts, clean diagnostic versions, actual image inspection and bounded revisions—must support the selected story rather than guarantee quality. Later nature/scatter/fog research is separate. Software tests cannot establish accurate example optics, artistic acceptance or temporal continuity.
